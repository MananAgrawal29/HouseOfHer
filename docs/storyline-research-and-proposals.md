# House Of Her — Storyline Research & Concept Proposals

**Document status:** Exploratory / not approved  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-11-08

> This document records the current creative vision and five possible narrative directions. None is the final canon. The protagonist's backstory, Her's identity, the central event, the ending, and the final twist remain open.

## 1. Project vision

House Of Her is intended to be a psychological horror experience, not merely a horror game with a story attached. Its purpose is to make the player care about the protagonist, inhabit his experience, question what is real, and reach an ending that changes the meaning of earlier events.

The house is the primary environment. It should feel lived-in, specific, believable, and narratively meaningful. Artwork, photographs, lighting, furniture, carpets, doors, corridors, electrical fixtures, sounds, and small environmental details should contribute to atmosphere or storytelling.

"Her" is deliberately undefined. She may not need a visible body, face, or even a conventional physical presence. The uncertainty around what Her is may be central to the experience.

## 2. Ideas established so far

These are ideas raised during concept discussions. They are creative directions, not all confirmed production requirements.

- An introductory cinematic should establish the protagonist before player control begins.
- The protagonist should be emotionally understandable before the player understands the mystery.
- The player should believe the protagonist has gone to sleep and then genuinely woken into his ordinary life. The game must not announce or visually label the apparent waking as a dream.
- The apparent timeline should feel continuous, potentially spanning what seems like multiple days. Explicit dream labels or repeated obvious wake-up reveals would undermine the intended deception.
- Cinematics may show the protagonist crying or remembering fragments of his ordinary life outside the apparent experience. At first, these should read as memories or unresolved emotions, not proof of a dream.
- Hallucinations and reality distortions should range from subtle anomalies to major sequences. A house-fire sequence, for example, may initially seem catastrophic before the player discovers it was a hallucination.
- The power outage and fuse-restoration mechanic remains part of the gameplay direction. It should serve atmosphere, tension, exploration, and possibly story progression—not feel like an unrelated timer.
- The protagonist may visit a relative. The visit could expose guilt, memories, family history, or information that changes the player's understanding.
- Real-world sounds during sleep could potentially influence what the protagonist experiences: knocking, footsteps, voices, a phone ringing, or other environmental sounds might be transformed or exaggerated inside the game. This is an experimental feature, not a guaranteed implementation. Any microphone use must be explicit, local where possible, privacy-preserving, and never capture or transmit private conversations.
- The protagonist's suicide was proposed as a possible event that ends the dream and leads to waking. This is **not approved canon**. It requires careful ethical and narrative evaluation; it must not be presented as heroic, liberating, or a solution to suffering. Alternative endings should be explored.
- The story should contain a substantial twist and emotional payoff. The reveal should be surprising but retrospectively supported by clues.
- The player should be able to reconsider earlier scenes after the ending and notice details whose meaning has changed.

## 3. Storytelling principles

1. **Emotional investment before explanation.** Let the player understand the protagonist emotionally before revealing the full truth.
2. **Fair misdirection.** Hide information, but do not cheat. The clues should exist before the reveal.
3. **The house tells the story.** Environmental details should carry meaning, not merely decorate rooms.
4. **Fear through uncertainty.** Use anticipation, isolation, sound, helplessness, and changing expectations—not only jump scares.
5. **Her remains a mystery.** Avoid prematurely reducing Her to a conventional ghost or monster.
6. **Dreams are not a universal excuse.** The apparent dream must have internal consistency; not every inconsistency can be dismissed with "it was a dream."
7. **The twist must have a payoff.** A surprising explanation is insufficient unless it deepens the emotional meaning of the experience.
8. **Gameplay and narrative reinforce each other.** Exploration, fuse restoration, object interaction, audio, lighting, and cinematics should belong to the same experience.

## 4. Reference analysis: techniques to study

The following are creative reference points. The lessons below are high-level observations, not claims that House Of Her should reproduce their plots.

### Memento (2000)
**Useful techniques:** fragmented chronology, unreliable memory, selective information, physical evidence, and making the audience form and revise hypotheses.

**Application:** photographs, notes, recordings, objects, and apparently contradictory clues could help the player reconstruct a truth. Any evidence system should remain emotionally grounded rather than becoming a detached detective minigame.

**Caution:** do not copy its memory condition, signature structure, or specific plot devices.

### Shutter Island (2010)
**Useful techniques:** a seemingly coherent investigation, controlled point of view, planted inconsistencies, character psychology, and a reveal that recontextualizes prior scenes.

**Application:** the protagonist's interpretation of events can guide the player while small details quietly challenge it. A final revelation should explain why the player accepted the earlier interpretation.

**Caution:** avoid using mental illness as a shortcut for a villain reveal or treating psychological distress as inherently dangerous.

### Jacob's Ladder (1990)
**Useful techniques:** destabilized reality, disturbing transitions, recurring imagery, existential unease, and horror that gains meaning through the protagonist's inner experience.

**Application:** hallucinations can be emotionally and symbolically connected to the protagonist rather than being random frightening images.

**Caution:** surrealism needs a coherent emotional logic, even when literal explanations remain ambiguous.

### The Others (2001)
**Useful techniques:** enclosed setting, sound-led suspense, controlled visibility, domestic routine, and carefully planted details that support a major reinterpretation.

**Application:** the house can be frightening without constant activity. Ordinary rooms, doors, footsteps, silence, and household routines can carry suspense.

**Caution:** avoid copying its central reveal or distinctive character setup.

### Inception (2010)
**Useful techniques:** layered realities, consistent rules for altered states, transitions between levels, and ambiguity around certainty.

**Application:** establish subtle rules for how the apparent reality behaves. The audience need not know the rules immediately, but the writers should.

**Caution:** avoid turning the story into a technical puzzle about dream mechanics at the expense of character and emotion.

### The Babadook (2014)
**Useful techniques:** a threatening presence connected to emotional pain, domestic horror, escalating tension, and a monster whose meaning extends beyond its literal appearance.

**Application:** Her may represent or connect to something the protagonist cannot face, without requiring a simplistic one-to-one metaphor.

**Caution:** retain Her's mystery and avoid reducing the story to a familiar "monster equals grief" explanation.

### Doraemon: Nobita's Three Visionary Swordsmen (1994)
**Useful techniques to examine:** dream-world continuity, the relationship between ordinary life and dream experience, and how a dream premise can support a sustained adventure.

**Application:** use it as a reference for dream-world storytelling, then transform the tone and narrative purpose for psychological horror.

**Caution:** the player's experience should not openly announce that the protagonist is dreaming.

### Silent Hill 2 (2001)
**Useful techniques:** psychological horror integrated into game structure, symbolic environments, protagonist-centred mystery, guilt, and emotional revelations delivered through exploration and character encounters.

**Application:** make the house and gameplay spaces express the protagonist's inner conflict. Let the player discover narrative information through actions and spaces, not only exposition.

**Caution:** avoid borrowing its character relationships, imagery, monsters, or central plot.

## 5. Five original storyline directions

These are expandable premises, not completed plots. The central event, exact twist, character biographies, and ending still need development.

### Concept 01 — The Life That Never Happened
**Genre:** psychological horror, mystery, emotional thriller  
**Themes:** regret, guilt, alternate lives, the desire to undo the past.

The protagonist appears to wake into an ordinary life. The player accepts the routine and relationships as real. He visits a relative, and the visit brings forward guilt, memories, and emotional reactions that do not quite fit the life the player has seen. Hallucinations intensify; the house may appear to burn, only for the destruction to vanish.

**Possible direction for the reveal:** the apparent life is a dream that never ended when the protagonist seemed to wake. Its relationships and events have emotional connections to his actual life. Waking in the real world forces him to respond differently to something he had taken for granted.

**Strengths:** closest to the original concept; strong emotional potential; supports cutscenes, family relationships, environmental storytelling, and real-world audio influence.

**Risks:** "it was all a dream" is familiar. The story needs a deeper discovery and a meaningful reason for this particular dream.

### Concept 02 — The House Remembers
**Genre:** supernatural psychological horror, mystery  
**Themes:** memory, suppressed guilt, identity, the past.

The house behaves as if it remembers events the protagonist cannot. Photographs, paintings, doors, rooms, sounds, and objects change or reveal details. Her appears to haunt the property, but never needs to manifest physically. A relative may know more than they initially reveal.

**Possible direction for the reveal:** Her is connected to a person, memory, or emotional truth the protagonist has suppressed. The house reconstructs a past event he cannot face. The player discovers that the apparent haunting has been built around a central event in his real history.

**Strengths:** makes the house a core narrative device; gives Her a central role; supports exploration and environmental clues.

**Risks:** the supernatural explanation could become generic. The central event and Her's connection must be distinctive.

### Concept 03 — The Morning That Never Came
**Genre:** psychological horror, existential mystery, dream thriller  
**Themes:** time, denial, fear of loss, consciousness and reality.

The protagonist seems to wake and continue ordinary life along a continuous timeline. Repeated sounds, familiar conversations, impossible times, missing intervals, and emotional memories create subtle inconsistencies. The game never labels separate dream sequences.

**Possible direction for the reveal:** the protagonist never reached the morning after the initial night. The apparent days form an extended dream in which the mind repeatedly reconstructs reality. An early detail becomes the key to understanding the entire experience.

**Strengths:** strongest fit for a continuous hidden-dream structure; enables subtle foreshadowing and a powerful opening-to-ending callback.

**Risks:** the dream reveal alone may be predictable. It needs a second layer of meaning.

### Concept 04 — The Man Who Couldn't Remember
**Genre:** psychological mystery, investigative horror  
**Themes:** memory manipulation, self-deception, guilt, unreliable narratives.

The protagonist discovers unfamiliar photographs, notes, recordings, and moved objects. He documents events to understand what is happening. The player investigates alongside him, but different sources contradict one another. Some evidence may have been created or altered by the protagonist.

**Possible direction for the reveal:** the protagonist has constructed a version of events that protects him from a truth he cannot accept. The investigation is exposing contradictions in his own account. The dream structure could be the mechanism through which he repeatedly reconstructs and distorts his memories.

**Strengths:** strongest potential for player participation in the mystery; gives collectibles and clues a meaningful purpose; supports retrospective revelations.

**Risks:** could become an investigation game with horror attached. Character and emotional stakes must remain central.

### Concept 05 — Her
**Genre:** psychological horror, existential mystery, emotional thriller  
**Themes:** identity, attachment, guilt, perception, reality.

The protagonist experiences unexplained events and fragmented memories. Her is present through sounds, environmental changes, memories, and emotional reactions. Sometimes he seems to recognise her, sometimes fear her, and sometimes miss her. The player tries to understand what she is.

**Possible direction for the reveal:** Her is the central connection between the protagonist's actual life, memories, and apparent reality. The protagonist has misunderstood his relationship with Her. The title, the house, and the recurring presence acquire a new meaning at the end.

**Strengths:** strongest opportunity for a distinctive identity and an ambiguous central mystery.

**Risks:** currently the least concrete. It needs a central event, clear character stakes, and a satisfying explanation.

## 6. Potential combinations

### A — The emotional mystery
Combine 01 + 02. An apparently ordinary alternative life gradually reveals a connection between the protagonist, the house, and someone he has lost, hurt, or failed.

### B — The mind-bending mystery
Combine 03 + 04. A continuous apparent timeline becomes an investigation into the protagonist's unreliable memories. The investigation itself may be part of the distortion.

### C — The identity mystery
Combine 02 + 05. The house appears haunted by Her, but the investigation reveals a deeply personal connection between Her and the protagonist.

### D — The complete psychological experience
Combine 01 + 02 + 04 + 05. An emotionally constructed alternative life, an unreliable investigation, a house that appears to remember the past, and a mysterious presence connected to the protagonist.

This is the most ambitious combination, not automatically the best one. Complexity should only be retained when it strengthens clarity, emotion, and payoff.

## 7. Comparison

| Concept | Main strength | Main challenge |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — The Life That Never Happened | Emotional impact | Avoiding a predictable dream twist |
| 02 — The House Remembers | Environmental storytelling | Original supernatural explanation |
| 03 — The Morning That Never Came | Continuous reality distortion | Giving the reveal another layer |
| 04 — The Man Who Couldn't Remember | Mind-bending mystery | Keeping horror and emotion central |
| 05 — Her | Identity and originality | Developing a concrete plot |

## 8. Questions for the next development phase

1. Who is the protagonist in his actual life?
2. What is his central emotional conflict?
3. Who or what is Her?
4. Why does the protagonist experience the apparent reality?
5. What happened before the opening scene?
6. Why is the house important?
7. What is the significance of the relative?
8. What is the purpose of the hallucinations?
9. What does the player discover that the protagonist does not understand?
10. What should the player feel during the final scene?

These questions should be answered after selecting and expanding a direction, not all at once.

## 9. Ethical and production considerations

- The proposed suicide ending is unapproved. Evaluate alternatives. Do not frame suicide as a heroic escape, liberation, or solution to suffering.
- Real-world audio integration must be optional, transparent, and privacy-preserving. Do not record or transmit private conversations. A fallback experience must work without microphone access.
- Hallucinations should have narrative purpose and variation; avoid making every strange event a predictable jump scare.
- The dream premise needs internal rules. Not every inconsistency should be excused by the final reveal.
- The house should remain small enough for the project's hardware constraints while being richly detailed.
- Story decisions should be made before building elaborate systems around unapproved plot assumptions.

## 10. Current recommendation

The strongest foundations to investigate further are:

1. **The House Remembers** — best foundation for the house and Her as the central identity of the project.
2. **The Man Who Couldn't Remember** — strongest potential for a player-driven, mind-bending mystery.
3. **The Life That Never Happened** — closest to the original dream concept and strongest immediate emotional direction.

A hybrid of these may ultimately be the strongest route, but it should be tested for coherence before approval.

**The target is a story that makes the player want to replay the experience after finishing because they now understand that earlier scenes meant something different.**
